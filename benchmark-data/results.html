Flow control benchmark results

Priority, fairness, reserved capacity, and eviction let realtime, batch, and tenant traffic share one model pool.

Takeaways

These measurements depend on the model, GPU, request shape, and traffic. Compare runs that used the same setup.

Share one GPU pool

Chat, batch, and tenant traffic can share one GPU instead of separate underutilized pools.

Priority and fairness preserved preferential access for realtime traffic during the tested surges.

Protect higher-priority traffic

When the pool saturates, policy decides who waits. Higher-priority traffic is protected under load, while lower-priority traffic absorbs the queue’s latency.

The charts below show that priority order during the tested surges.

Keep peers receiving service

One tenant’s surge does not starve same-priority peers.

Round-robin fairness kept peer tenants receiving dispatch turns inside the shared priority band.

Protect realtime after batch starts

Reserved capacity and eviction protect real-time p95 TTFT while batch sequences are already executing in vLLM.

Evicted batch requests were retried, and each tested job produced one final result.
